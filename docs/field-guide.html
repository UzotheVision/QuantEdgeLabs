Where ideas come from

The scanners, and what can stop them

Every card is published by one of these. Each has an evidence bar it must clear — and a veto that can block it even when the pattern looks perfect.

ScannerIt publishes when…It is blocked when…
Aggressor tape$8M+ of options premium is bought (long) or sold (short) in one name today — or $5M+ with the chart independently confirming.The flow is two-sided, or the name was already carded today.
Bottom reversalA stock that fell 10%+ prints a dated bottom and then a higher-lows base or a sharp V-recovery. Liquid names only.The options tape leans short against it.
Leader swingA benchmark name (mega caps, semis, software, optics…) pulls back 2–9% while above a rising 50-day average.Big money is selling the dip.
Index swingSPY, QQQ, IWM, SMH, IGV… pull back 1.2–6% inside an uptrend.The index is in a downtrend or selling hard for days.
Premium discountSPY/QQQ weekly options are priced below what the index has actually been moving (implied vol under realized).The index is below its trend.
Crypto transmissionBTC or ETH moves 6%+ in a week and a proxy (COIN, MSTR, IBIT…) shows its own buying.The proxy's own tape doesn't confirm — the coin moving is never enough.

What the bands mean

SMany layers agree, nothing strong argues against. The best the board has.
ASolid evidence, a minor objection or two. Tradeable.
BWorth watching. Wait for the trigger and the tape.
CThe engine is saying “meh.” Usually a skip.

“Your contract, repriced now”

When an idea is published it picks a contract. Markets move after that — so the idea screen re-reads that exact contract from the options chain every two minutes and recalculates everything that drifts. Numbers from the moment of publishing are never shown as if they were current.

FieldHow to read it
Since publishHow much the premium has moved. +30% or more gets a “late entry” warning — much of the move is already paid for.
Expected moveHow far the options market expects the stock could move (one standard deviation) by expiry.
T1 distanceThe target measured in those units. Under 1σ is a normal ask; over 1σ means the target is beyond what the market is pricing.
Odds touch T1The market-implied chance price trades at T1 before expiry. It's what options are pricing, not a forecast.
If T1 todayWhat the contract would be worth if the target hit right now. Every day of waiting costs theta.
At stop todayWhat you'd likely get back if the stop hits now — your real risk on the contract.
Breakeven @expWhere the stock must finish for the contract to break even at expiry. If T1 doesn't clear it, the trade needs T1 early.

Picking a contract you can afford

Open any idea and scroll to Fit my budget. Choose how much you can spend per contract (≤$100 up to any) and how many days to expiry. It lists contracts that can realistically reach the target, with the estimated return if the stock gets there — and warns you plainly when time decay, a wide spread or thin volume will work against you.

Cheap and short pays the most but needs the move fast. More days costs more but survives a choppy week. The panel shows the trade-off instead of hiding it.

House rules the engine enforces

Long and short, same standard. Shorts need the same measured evidence as longs — heavy selling on the tape, a real breakdown. A card that fights the tape in either direction loses points.

Flow can veto — and flip. A pattern that looks great while big money trades against it gets blocked. When the tape flips on a name, the old opposite-side card is retired so you never see a long and a short side by side.

Nothing enters overnight. Ideas found after the close publish as triggers above the last price, never as trades already taken.

Delayed data is labelled. If a number isn't live, the screen says so.

Glossary

Aggressor tape
Options trades classified by whether the buyer paid the ask (bullish intent) or the seller hit the bid. Measured from real fills.
Pending trigger
An idea waiting for price to reach its entry during market hours. Not a trade until then.
R:R and 2R
Reward divided by risk. 2R means the target is twice as far from entry as the stop.
Delta
How much the contract moves per $1 in the stock. 0.40 ≈ 40¢ per dollar.
Theta
What the contract loses per day just from time passing. Shown as % of its price.
IV (implied volatility)
How much movement the option price assumes. High IV = expensive premium.
IV vs realized
Implied vol compared with how much the stock actually moved lately. Under 1.0 = premium is cheap for the movement you're getting.
σ (sigma)
One expected move. A target 0.5σ away is well inside normal range; 1.5σ is a stretch.
Gamma pin / call wall / put wall
Strikes where dealer hedging tends to pull or cap price. The GEX tab maps them.
Band (S/A/B/C)
The idea's evidence grade. Red chips on a card subtract from it.

Honest limits

Options prices are delayed about 15 minutes until the live data feed is reconnected. Every panel that uses them says so. Don't use it to time a 0DTE entry to the second.

Odds are market-implied, not predictions. They assume no drift and come from the option prices themselves.

Patterns are scored by their record. Every card's outcome is tracked. Scanners that don't pay over time get demoted by their own numbers.

Scanners run on a schedule. A stock that rips between sweeps shows up at the next one, not instantly.
